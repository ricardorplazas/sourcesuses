## For investment managers

A **high-water-mark provision** is a clause commonly found in performance-based compensation structures for investment managers, such as hedge funds, private equity funds, or mutual funds. This provision ensures that a fund manager only earns performance fees on net new profits, preventing them from receiving fees on the recovery of previous losses.

**Key Aspects of a High-Water-Mark Provision**:

1. **Definition of High-Water Mark**:
   - The **high-water mark** represents the highest net asset value (NAV) that a fund has achieved on which performance fees have been paid.
   - It serves as a benchmark that the fund must exceed before the manager can earn additional performance fees.

2. **Purpose**:
   - **Aligns Interests**: It aligns the interests of fund managers with those of investors by ensuring managers are incentivized to recover losses before earning additional fees.
   - **Prevents Double Dipping**: Managers cannot earn performance fees on gains that merely recover previous losses.

3. **How It Works**:
   - **Initial Investment**: Suppose an investor puts in $1,000,000, and the fund's NAV increases to $1,200,000. The manager earns a performance fee on the $200,000 profit.
   - **Subsequent Loss**: If the NAV then drops to $900,000 due to market losses, the high-water mark remains at $1,200,000.
   - **Recovery Phase**: The fund must surpass the $1,200,000 high-water mark before the manager can earn performance fees again.
   - **New Profits**: Once the NAV exceeds $1,200,000, say to $1,300,000, the manager earns fees only on the $100,000 gain above the high-water mark.

4. **Benefits to Investors**:
   - **Protection Against Paying for Recoveries**: Investors don't pay performance fees during periods when the fund is merely recouping previous losses.
   - **Encourages Long-Term Performance**: Managers are motivated to achieve sustainable growth rather than short-term gains followed by losses.

5. **Considerations**:
   - **Risk-Taking Behavior**: Managers might be tempted to take on higher risks to surpass the high-water mark.
   - **Duration of High-Water Mark**: In some cases, the high-water mark can reset after a certain period, or "soft" high-water marks may be used.
   - **Fee Structures**: The specifics can vary based on the fund's fee agreement, so it's essential to understand the terms outlined in the investment contract.

**Example Scenario**:

- **Year 1**:
  - Initial Investment: $1,000,000
  - Year-End NAV: $1,100,000
  - Profit: $100,000
  - Performance Fee (e.g., 20%): Manager earns $20,000
  - High-Water Mark Set at: $1,100,000

- **Year 2**:
  - Year-End NAV: $1,050,000
  - Loss: $50,000
  - Performance Fee: $0
  - High-Water Mark Remains at: $1,100,000

- **Year 3**:
  - Year-End NAV: $1,080,000
  - Gain: $30,000
  - Since NAV is below the high-water mark, no performance fee is earned.
  - High-Water Mark Remains at: $1,100,000

- **Year 4**:
  - Year-End NAV: $1,150,000
  - Gain Above High-Water Mark: $50,000
  - Performance Fee (20% of $50,000): Manager earns $10,000
  - New High-Water Mark Set at: $1,150,000

**Conclusion**:

A high-water-mark provision is a crucial feature in investment management contracts that protects investors from paying performance fees during periods of recovery after losses. It ensures that fund managers are compensated only when they deliver net new gains above the previous peak performance, thereby aligning their incentives with the long-term interests of the investors.


## In a loan

In the context of a **syndicated loan**, a **high-water-mark provision** refers to a clause in the loan agreement that sets specific thresholds or maximum limits ("high-water marks") on certain financial metrics, loan utilization levels, or borrower actions. When these thresholds are reached or exceeded, it triggers predefined consequences such as adjustments to interest rates, fees, or covenants. This provision helps lenders manage risk and ensures that borrowers maintain certain financial standards throughout the loan term.

---

**Key Aspects of High-Water-Mark Provisions in Syndicated Loans**:

1. **Interest Rate Adjustments Based on Financial Metrics**:
   - **Performance-Based Pricing**: The loan's interest rate margin may fluctuate depending on the borrower's financial performance metrics, such as leverage ratios or credit ratings.
   - **High-Water Mark Thresholds**: If the borrower's leverage ratio exceeds a specified high-water mark (e.g., debt-to-EBITDA ratio of 3.0x), the interest rate margin increases, resulting in higher interest costs.

2. **Utilization Fees Tied to Loan Usage**:
   - **Fee Implementation**: Additional fees may be imposed when the borrower utilizes the loan beyond a certain percentage of the total commitment.
   - **Threshold Levels**: For example, if loan utilization exceeds 50% of the total facility, a utilization fee of 0.25% may apply to the amount above that threshold.

3. **Financial Covenants and Ratios**:
   - **Covenant Compliance**: High-water marks set maximum allowable levels for financial ratios. Exceeding these levels can lead to a covenant breach.
   - **Trigger Events**: Breaching a high-water mark in financial covenants may require the borrower to take corrective actions, provide additional collateral, or potentially face default.

4. **Restrictions on Additional Indebtedness**:
   - **Debt Incurrence Limits**: The provision may cap the amount of additional debt the borrower can incur, protecting lenders from increased risk.
   - **High-Water Mark Level**: Setting a maximum debt level (e.g., total debt not to exceed $500 million) serves as a high-water mark.

5. **Adjustments to Commitment Fees**:
   - **Fee Reductions or Increases**: Commitment fees on the undrawn portion of the loan may adjust based on utilization levels or credit ratings.
   - **Threshold-Based Changes**: If the borrower's credit rating improves beyond a certain point, commitment fees might decrease, and vice versa.

---

**Purpose of High-Water-Mark Provisions**:

- **Risk Mitigation**: Protect lenders by ensuring that the borrower's financial health remains within acceptable parameters.
- **Incentivizing Performance**: Encourage borrowers to maintain strong financial metrics to benefit from lower interest rates or fees.
- **Aligning Interests**: Align the borrower's financial management with the lenders' risk tolerance.

---

**Example Scenario**:

- **Loan Agreement Terms**:
  - **Total Loan Commitment**: $200 million
  - **Interest Rate Margin**: Initially LIBOR + 2.0%
  - **Leverage Ratio High-Water Marks**:
    - **≤ 2.5x**: Interest margin remains at LIBOR + 2.0%
    - **> 2.5x and ≤ 3.5x**: Interest margin increases to LIBOR + 2.5%
    - **> 3.5x**: Interest margin increases to LIBOR + 3.0%

- **Utilization Fee**:
  - **Applies When Utilization > 60%**: Additional 0.25% fee on the utilized amount above 60% of the total commitment.

- **Borrower's Financial Performance**:
  - **Year 1**: Leverage ratio is 2.0x, utilization is 50%—no changes in interest rate or fees.
  - **Year 2**: Leverage ratio increases to 3.0x, utilization is 65%.
    - **Interest Margin Adjustment**: Increases to LIBOR + 2.5% due to exceeding the 2.5x leverage high-water mark.
    - **Utilization Fee Applied**: 0.25% fee on the amount above 60% utilization.

---

**Considerations for Borrowers and Lenders**:

- **Negotiation of Terms**: High-water-mark provisions are critical negotiation points. Borrowers aim for flexibility, while lenders seek protection.
- **Monitoring and Compliance**: Both parties must actively monitor financial metrics to manage compliance and anticipate adjustments.
- **Clear Definitions**: Precise definitions of financial metrics and calculation methods are essential to prevent disputes.

---

**Conclusion**:

In syndicated loans, high-water-mark provisions are mechanisms that tie the loan's terms—such as interest rates, fees, and covenants—to specific financial thresholds. These provisions help manage risk by adjusting the loan conditions when the borrower's financial situation changes, ensuring that lenders are compensated for increased risk and borrowers are incentivized to maintain strong financial health.

**By incorporating high-water-mark provisions, syndicated loan agreements create a dynamic structure that responds to the borrower's financial performance, aligning the interests of both borrowers and lenders throughout the life of the loan.**
