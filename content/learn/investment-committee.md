# What belongs in a five-slide IC presentation

**Intent:** compress a case into a decision-led presentation. **Reviewed:** 16 July 2026.

Five slides are enough when each slide answers a distinct investment question. The structure below is a constraint, not a mandatory house style.

## Slide 1 — recommendation and key terms

State the decision, conditions, entry valuation, leverage and base/downside return range. List the two or three facts that most influence the decision. Do not spend the opening slide on company history.

## Slide 2 — business quality

Explain demand durability, customer behaviour, competitive position and the operating capacity needed to deliver. Choose evidence that distinguishes high-quality revenue from merely repeated revenue.

## Slide 3 — underwriting and value creation

Bridge historical performance to the plan. Show which portion comes from volume, price, mix, productivity, acquisition or cost reduction. Identify what is already evidenced and what is an underwriting assumption.

## Slide 4 — leverage, returns and downside

Present the enterprise-to-equity bridge, debt development, cash conversion and return sensitivities. The downside should change operating performance and financing capacity coherently—not just reduce the exit multiple.

## Slide 5 — risks and decision-changing diligence

Rank risks by investment impact. For each, identify current evidence, the unanswered question and what an adverse finding does to price, structure or recommendation.

## The consistency audit

Run five comparisons before submitting:

1. Slide-one returns equal the model.
2. The value-creation bridge explains the operating case.
3. The downside narrative matches the downside model.
4. Risks are reflected in conditions or diligence.
5. The final recommendation has not drifted from the opening slide.

This is an original educational structure, not a copied investment-committee template.
