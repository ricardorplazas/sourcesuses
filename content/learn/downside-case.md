# Building a useful downside case

**Intent:** test how the investment fails, not merely produce a lower IRR. **Reviewed:** 16 July 2026.

A useful downside begins with a causal story. Example: price increases do not hold, hiring remains slow and customer churn rises after service levels weaken. These drivers affect revenue, margin, working capital, cash generation and debt paydown together.

## Four layers

1. **Trigger:** what adverse event or evidence changes?
2. **Operating transmission:** how do volume, price, mix or capacity respond?
3. **Cash and financing:** what happens to cash conversion, covenant headroom and debt?
4. **Decision consequence:** does the answer change at the same price and structure?

## Avoid the exit-multiple-only downside

An exit multiple sensitivity is useful, but it does not test operational resilience. Combine it with coherent operating assumptions and show the bridge from weaker performance to lower equity value.

## Make the downside actionable

Conclude with the mitigation, diligence or transaction term that addresses the failure mode. If no practical mitigation exists, say so.
