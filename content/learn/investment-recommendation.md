# How to structure a one-page investment recommendation

**Intent:** turn analysis into a decision rather than a company description. **Reviewed:** 16 July 2026.

A useful one-page recommendation answers six questions in descending order of decision importance.

## 1. What should the firm do?

Lead with one of three positions: proceed, proceed subject to stated conditions, or decline. Include the valuation or financing boundary when it changes the answer.

Weak: “The company operates in an attractive market and could be an interesting opportunity.”

Stronger: “Proceed to confirmatory diligence at no more than the indicated valuation, subject to validating price retention, technician capacity and the top-customer renewal.”

## 2. Why might this be a good investment?

Use two to four evidence-linked thesis points. Each should connect a business mechanism to an economic consequence. “Large market” is not enough; identify why the company can capture profitable demand.

## 3. What must be true?

Expose the underwriting dependencies: price, volume, capacity, margins, cash conversion, leverage and exit. Distinguish supplied facts from your assumptions.

## 4. How does the downside fail?

Describe interactions rather than isolated sensitivities. For example, slower hiring can constrain revenue, prevent margin leverage and reduce debt paydown at the same time.

## 5. What evidence argues against the recommendation?

Include disconfirming evidence. This is not self-sabotage; it demonstrates calibrated confidence and makes diligence priorities concrete.

## 6. What happens next?

Name the two or three questions that can change the decision, who or what can answer them, and the consequence of an adverse answer.

## One-page structure

```text
Recommendation and conditions
Key terms and base/downside returns
Investment thesis
Principal risks and disconfirming evidence
Value-creation plan
Decision-changing diligence priorities
```

Before submitting, verify that the recommendation uses the same EBITDA definition, debt balance, entry valuation, exit assumption and returns outputs as the model.

This is an original editorial framework and not a firm-specific memo template.
