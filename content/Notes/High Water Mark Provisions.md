> [!abstract] Definition
> A High-Water Mark Provision is a clause establishing a peak value or threshold, primarily used within [[Investment Management]] fee structures and [[Syndicated Loan]] agreements. It sets a benchmark (the "high-water mark") that must be surpassed before certain consequences are triggered, such as the payment of [[Performance Fees]] to fund managers or adjustments to loan terms like [[Interest Rate|Interest Rates]] or [[Covenants]].

## In Investment Management (Performance Fees)

### Definition & Purpose
* In the context of [[Investment Management]] (e.g., [[Hedge Funds]], [[Private Equity]]), this provision ensures managers only earn [[Performance Fees]] on *new* profits that increase the fund's [[Net Asset Value (NAV)]] above its highest previous value for which fees were paid.
* **Purpose**:
    * **Aligns Interests**: Connects manager compensation directly to generating net new gains for investors.
    * **Prevents Double Dipping**: Avoids charging fees on gains that merely recover prior losses.

### How it Works
1.  **Initial High-Water Mark**: Set at the initial investment NAV or the NAV after the last performance fee payment.
2.  **Performance Fee Trigger**: Fees are only earned on profits that push the NAV *above* the current high-water mark.
3.  **Loss Periods**: If the NAV drops below the high-water mark, no performance fees are earned until the NAV recovers and surpasses that previous peak.
4.  **New High-Water Mark**: Once fees are paid on new profits, the high-water mark is reset to the new, higher NAV level.

### Key Aspects & Benefits for Investors
* **Investor Protection**: Investors don't pay performance fees while the fund is recovering losses.
* **Long-Term Focus**: Motivates managers towards sustainable growth rather than volatile short-term gains.
* **Considerations**:
    * Can potentially incentivize excessive [[Risk Management|risk-taking]] if a manager is far below the mark.
    * Terms may include reset periods or variations ("soft" high-water marks).

### Example
* **Year 1**: Invest $1M -> NAV $1.1M. Profit $100k. Fee paid. HWM set at $1.1M.
* **Year 2**: NAV drops to $1.05M. Loss. No fee. HWM remains $1.1M.
* **Year 3**: NAV rises to $1.08M. Gain, but still below HWM. No fee. HWM remains $1.1M.
* **Year 4**: NAV rises to $1.15M. Gain above HWM = $50k ($1.15M - $1.1M). Fee paid on $50k. New HWM set at $1.15M.

## In Syndicated Loans (Loan Terms)

### Definition & Purpose
* In a [[Syndicated Loan]] context, this provision sets specific thresholds (high-water marks) for financial metrics (like [[Leverage Ratio]]), loan utilization, or borrower actions.
* **Purpose**:
    * **[[Risk Management]]**: Protects lenders by adjusting terms if borrower risk increases (e.g., higher leverage).
    * **Incentivizes Performance**: Encourages borrowers to maintain strong financials to secure better loan terms (e.g., lower [[Interest Rate|Interest Rates]]).
    * **Aligns Interests**: Links loan conditions dynamically to the borrower's financial health, aligning lender and borrower goals.

### Common Applications
* **Performance-Based Pricing**: [[Interest Rate]] margins adjust based on metrics like the Debt-to-[[EBITDA]] [[Leverage Ratio]]. Exceeding a high-water mark ratio triggers a higher margin.
* **Utilization Fees**: Additional fees may apply if the drawn amount on a credit facility exceeds a certain percentage (the high-water mark) of the total commitment.
* **[[Financial Covenants]]**: High-water marks define maximum allowable levels for key ratios. Breaching these can trigger default clauses or require corrective action.
* **Debt Incurrence Limits**: Caps the total amount of debt a borrower can take on.

### Example
* **Loan Term**: Interest Rate = Base + 2.0% if [[Leverage Ratio]] ≤ 2.5x (HWM 1); Base + 2.5% if Ratio > 2.5x and ≤ 3.5x (HWM 2); Base + 3.0% if Ratio > 3.5x.
* **Scenario**: Borrower's leverage ratio increases from 2.0x to 3.0x.
* **Consequence**: The interest rate margin increases from 2.0% to 2.5% because the 2.5x high-water mark was exceeded.
