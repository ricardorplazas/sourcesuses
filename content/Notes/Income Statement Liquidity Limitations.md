> [!abstract] Summary
> While the [[Income Statement]] measures [[Accrual Accounting|accrual-based]] profitability over a period, it can be insufficient or even misleading when assessing a company's actual [[Liquidity]] or cash position.

## Key Reasons

1.  **Timing Mismatch (Accrual vs. Cash)**: Revenue is recognized when earned, not necessarily when cash is received. A company can show high [[Net Income]] but struggle if customers don't pay (high [[Accounts Receivable]]).
2.  **Non-Cash Items**: The IS includes significant non-cash expenses like [[Depreciation & Amortization (D&A)]] that impact [[Net Income]] but don't represent actual cash outflows during the period.
3.  **Management Discretion**: Accrual accounting allows for management judgment (e.g., useful life assumptions, allowance for doubtful accounts), which can be used to manage earnings and may not perfectly reflect the underlying cash reality.

## Complementary Statement

The [[Cash Flow Statement]] is essential as it reconciles [[Net Income]] to the actual change in cash by adjusting for non-cash items and changes in working capital, providing a clearer view of liquidity.
