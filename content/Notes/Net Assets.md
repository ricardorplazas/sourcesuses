> [!abstract] Definition
> Net Assets represent the total assets of a company minus its total liabilities, essentially reflecting the book value of equity. In private equity and M&A transactions, this figure forms the foundation for assessing the company's balance sheet strength.

Net Assets are a key metric in determining the baseline value of a business. They include both [[Tangible Assets|tangible assets]] (like [[Property, Plant & Equipment|property, plant, and equipment]]) and identifiable [[Intangible Assets|intangible assets]] (such as [[Patents|patents]] and [[Trademarks|trademarks]]), less any [[Liabilities|liabilities]]. This value is critical during [[due diligence]] and [[Entry Valuation|valuation processes]], as it helps investors understand the net worth of the target company before additional adjustments like [[Write-up of Assets|asset write-ups]] or the recognition of [[Goodwill|goodwill]].

---

## Key Components

- **[[Tangible Assets]]**: Physical assets such as buildings, machinery, and [[Inventory|inventory]].
- **[[Intangible Assets]]**: Identifiable non-physical assets, including [[Patents|patents]], [[Trademarks|trademarks]], and customer relationships.
- **[[Liabilities]]**: Obligations and [[Debt|debts]], including [[Short-Term Liability|short-term]] and [[Long-Term Liability|long-term liabilities]].

---

## Modeling in Transaction Analysis
In transaction models, Net Assets are typically calculated as follows:
1. **Start with the [[Balance Sheet]]**: Obtain the reported figures for total assets and total liabilities.
2. **Adjustments for Fair Value**: Revalue [[Balance Sheet Assets|Assets]] and [[Liabilities|liabilities]] to their fair market value to reflect the true economic position.
3. **[[Non-Recurring Items|Exclusion of Non-Core Items]]**: Sometimes non-recurring or non-operational items are excluded to focus on the core operating assets and liabilities.
4. **Derivation of [[Net Identifiable Assets]]**: The adjusted net asset value becomes the [[Net Identifiable Assets]] Valuation, a critical input for further calculations such as determining goodwill or equity step-up in a transaction.

Net Assets provide a clear picture of the company's existing equity and serve as a benchmark for assessing changes post-transaction, including the impact of strategic adjustments, financing decisions, or operational improvements.
