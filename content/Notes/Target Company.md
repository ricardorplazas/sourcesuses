> [!abstract] Definition
> A Target Company refers to a specific business or corporation that has been chosen for acquisition, investment, or takeover by another company (the Acquirer) or by investors (such as a private equity firm in a [[Leveraged Buyout|Leveraged Buyout (LBO)]]).

The target is the entity whose assets, operations, and potentially liabilities are being evaluated and pursued in a potential transaction. Identifying and analyzing suitable target companies is a core activity in [[Mergers & Acquisitions (M&A)]] and investment processes.

---
## Characteristics of an Attractive Target (Especially for Financial Sponsors / LBOs)

While attractiveness depends on the acquirer's strategy, certain traits often make a company a more desirable target, particularly for financial buyers seeking strong returns:

1.  **Stable and Predictable Cash Flows**: Sufficient cash generation to service acquisition [[Debt]], fund operations, and provide returns.
2.  **Strong Market Position / Defensible Niche**: Established brand, loyal customer base, high barriers to entry, sustainable competitive advantages.
3.  **Potential for Operational Improvement**: Opportunities for the acquirer to enhance value through cost reductions, efficiency gains, revenue growth initiatives, or strategic repositioning.
4.  **Strong Management Team (or Opportunity for Enhancement)**: Competent existing leadership, or the potential for the acquirer to install a new team to drive improvements.
5.  **Favorable Industry Trends**: Operating within a growing, resilient, or fragmented industry ripe for consolidation.
6.  **"Correct" Valuation / Entry Price**: Acquirable at a price that allows for potential upside and meeting the acquirer's return hurdles. Related to [[Valuation]] methodologies.
7.  **Viable [[Exit Strategy|Exit Strategies]]**: Clear potential pathways for the acquirer to eventually sell their investment and realize returns (e.g., IPO, sale to a strategic buyer, secondary buyout).
8.  **(LBO Specific) Strong Asset Base**: Tangible assets that can be used as collateral to secure acquisition financing ([[Debt]]).
