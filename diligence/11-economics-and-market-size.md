# Supporting analysis — Market size, revenue and unit economics

## Method and uncertainty

No authoritative dataset counts annual European PE interview-preparation buyers. The estimates below are bottom-up decision ranges, not market facts. They should be replaced by observed funnel and partner data.

Useful boundary evidence:

- Invest Europe estimates 29,338 active PE-backed portfolio companies in Europe in 2024; this establishes a substantial ecosystem but is not a candidate count.
- A London recruiter report profiles about 1,500 analysts and associates across investment banking, advisory, PE, institutional investors and professional services; this is one-city stock, not annual flow.
- London Business School alone welcomed 170 Masters in Finance students in 2025/26; the named target schools collectively create thousands of finance students, but only a minority actively pursue PE in a given year.
- Competitor self-reports range from 10,000+ Peak students to 56,763 BIWS paid accounts accumulated globally over many years. These demonstrate category demand, not European annual demand.

## TAM / SAM / SOM

- **Broad TAM:** 20,000–50,000 annual European/private-markets-interview learners across PE and adjacent roles × CHF 80–150 spend = roughly CHF 1.6m–7.5m annual category spend.
- **Initial SAM:** 6,000–15,000 English-speaking European PE candidates with a relevant case need × CHF 100–150 = roughly CHF 0.6m–2.25m annual spend.
- **Three-year realistic SOM:** 300–1,000 annual buyers × CHF 120–180 blended AOV after multiple cases = CHF 36k–180k gross annual revenue.

This is a credible side-business envelope. A standalone-company outcome requires either exceptional share, higher-ticket institutional licensing or an adjacent product with repeated use. It should not be assumed in the base case.

## Geographic concentration

Prioritise by reachable English-language candidate density:

1. London/UK;
2. Germany/Switzerland/Austria;
3. France and Benelux;
4. Italy and Spain;
5. Nordics;
6. international students at European schools.

London has the largest concentrated recruiter/fund/finance-school ecosystem. DACH offers founder credibility and mid-market relevance but needs German-language validation before localisation. France and Southern Europe have stronger local-language recruiting and school networks. Do not translate until English demand is proven.

## Seasonality

European PE recruitment is less uniformly “on-cycle” than the US and includes rolling/headhunter-led processes. Nevertheless, demand clusters around:

- autumn university recruiting and new analyst starts;
- January–May off-cycle/lateral processes;
- interview-specific spikes with only days/weeks of notice;
- lower activity during major summer and year-end holiday periods.

This supports evergreen search and partner distribution but weakens a monthly subscription. Use rolling 90-day results rather than one month for operating decisions.

## Base unit economics

Assumptions:

- blended AOV: CHF 95 initially;
- merchant-of-record/payment/fixed fee: 6.5%;
- refunds/chargebacks reserve: 4%;
- affiliate commission: 20% on 25% of sales = 5% blended;
- variable email/storage/delivery: 1.5%;
- contribution before fixed operating cost/founder time: **83% of gross** (rounded to 84% in scenario arithmetic).

Lemon Squeezy currently advertises 5% + $0.50 and no monthly processing fee. International/currency circumstances can add cost. Direct Stripe in Switzerland advertises 2.9% + CHF 0.30 domestic and 3.25% + CHF 0.30 international cards, but Stripe is not by itself merchant of record; early VAT/tax administration can outweigh the fee saving.

## University economics

Illustrative club licence:

- CHF 750 annual licence for 25 named/redemption seats = CHF 30/seat;
- two cases, no live workshop;
- one committee onboarding call and self-service codes;
- renewal only if at least 40% of allocated seats start a case.

If a club requires two bespoke workshops, logo customisation and repeated committee handholding, the price must exceed CHF 1,500–2,500 or the channel becomes a coaching/service business.

## Affiliate economics

Recommended initial terms:

- 20% of net product price, excluding VAT/refunds;
- 30-day last-click attribution with coupon override defined in writing;
- no self-referral or paid-search bidding on brand;
- payout after refund window, minimum threshold;
- disclosure required;
- terminate inactive partners after one academic year.

At CHF 79, a 20% commission is CHF 15.80. This can motivate clubs/small creators but leaves little room for paid acquisition plus refunds. Do not stack large discounts and commissions without contribution analysis.

## Paid acquisition

At CHF 95 AOV and roughly CHF 79 contribution before fixed costs, a safe early customer-acquisition cost ceiling is CHF 20–25, preserving room for content/support and uncertainty. Finance recruiting search terms are likely competitive, and low-volume European terms may not scale. Paid search/social should be tested only after:

- 20+ organic sales;
- landing-to-order conversion >=1%;
- refunds <=6%;
- 60-day contribution payback understood;
- functioning attribution.

Start with CHF 300–500 as a measurement test, not a growth plan.

## Does the add-in improve economics?

The add-in is attractive only if it raises blended AOV or premium mix enough to cover development/support. Illustrative hurdle:

- build/hardening opportunity cost: CHF 25k–50k equivalent;
- incremental contribution per premium buyer: assume CHF 50–80;
- buyers required to recover cost: roughly 315–1,000 before ongoing support.

That is a large share of the realistic three-year customer base. The add-in cannot be justified by differentiation rhetoric alone. A prototype-driven premium-choice test is essential.

## Standalone-company test

A standalone company becomes plausible around CHF 250k–400k annual gross revenue with durable partner/organic acquisition, >80% cash contribution before salaries, repeatable content production and limited founder support. That is above the base SOM and likely requires institutional licensing or adjacent products. The current evidence supports a side-business option, not a standalone-company underwriting case.
