## Job-to-be-done hierarchy

1. **Perform under time pressure.** Turn imperfect data into a defensible answer within the actual interview window.
2. **Know what “good” looks like.** See professional outputs and why one answer is stronger, not only a completed spreadsheet.
3. **Exercise judgment.** Identify commercial drivers, disconfirming evidence, downside cases and diligence priorities.
4. **Communicate concisely.** Produce a recommendation/memo/slides that an IC can act on.
5. **Avoid preventable execution errors.** Clean formulas, consistent formatting, sensible checks and submission readiness.
6. **Learn efficiently.** Find the smallest relevant practice sequence rather than consume a 100-video course.
7. **Gain confidence.** Reduce uncertainty before a high-value interview.

Definitions, accounting questions and basic paper LBOs are prerequisites, not the premium problem.

## Desired outcome

The buyer should finish able to say:

> I completed a realistic case in the allotted time, can explain the commercial and return drivers, know where my work fell short, and have a repeatable checklist for the real process.

Do not promise an offer, placement, certification value or recruiter recognition.

## Purchase decision

### Triggers

- interview invitation with known case/model stage;
- recruiter warning about a 2–4 hour test;
- first failed case round;
- university club case competition;
- transition from consulting/TS into a modeling-heavy process;
- incoming role with weak Excel confidence.

### Evidence required at checkout

- screenshots of the actual candidate experience;
- exact deliverables and time requirement;
- sample rubric excerpt;
- clear statement that every company and dataset is fictional/original;
- reviewer credentials described without employer endorsement;
- preview of model formatting/debrief quality;
- fair access and refund terms;
- Windows/Mac requirements for any Excel element.

### Reasons not to buy

- no interview is imminent;
- the candidate already owns Peak/WSO/BIWS;
- a colleague or employer supplies relevant cases;
- the buyer wants live feedback;
- free/AI-generated practice feels good enough;
- browser-only materials seem inconvenient;
- price exceeds the expected incremental confidence;
- unknown founder/brand lacks trust;
- candidate cannot install an add-in or use desktop Excel;
- concern that “European” is superficial marketing.

## Customer discovery questions

Avoid asking “Would you buy this?” Ask for evidence:

1. Walk me through the last case/model test you prepared for.
2. What materials did you actually use, and what did each cost?
3. Which part of the exercise surprised you?
4. Show the sequence in which you prepared during the final week.
5. Where did you receive feedback? What was missing?
6. What did you produce—model, memo, deck, oral recommendation—and under what time limit?
7. Which European differences materially changed the analysis?
8. When did you stop using a course/resource, and why?
9. Have you used AI for practice? Which outputs did you trust or reject?
10. Would a deterministic Excel productivity tool have been allowed in the test?
11. Rank a second full case, model review, live mock, Excel tool and recruiter guide by what you would buy next.
12. Ask the participant to choose between real checkout offers; do not rely on rating scales alone.

## Segment-specific implications

- **Bankers:** mechanics may be strong; product must emphasise investment judgment and ruthless prioritisation.
- **Consultants:** commercial reasoning may be strong; model scaffolding and returns/debt work need clearer sequencing.
- **Students:** require more prerequisites and lower price, but can be reached in groups.
- **Lateral/adjacent professionals:** highest need for integrated practice but smaller and heterogeneous.
- **Recruiters/firms:** need neutral, original, stable and supportable material; browser tracking can create data-protection/proctoring concerns.
- **Universities:** require accessibility, invoicing, repeatable administration and content that does not imply endorsement.
