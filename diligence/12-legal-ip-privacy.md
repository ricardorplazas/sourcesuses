# Supporting analysis — Legal, employment, IP, privacy and piracy

This document identifies issues for qualified advisers. It is not legal or tax advice.

## Pre-sale legal gate

Do not accept payment until a Swiss employment/IP lawyer or other appropriately qualified adviser has reviewed:

- employment contract and staff handbook;
- side-employment/board/business-activity approval requirement;
- duty of loyalty and non-compete/conflict language;
- confidentiality and post-employment obligations;
- invention/copyright/IP assignment language;
- permitted public biography and employer references;
- use of professional knowledge versus employer methods/materials;
- whether the proposed audience or product could be viewed as competitive.

Obtain written approval if required. Silence or informal manager awareness is not a substitute for a contractual process.

## Operational separation controls

- personal laptop, phone, storage, email, GitHub, payment and cloud accounts only;
- no employer time, software licences, AI accounts, printers, templates or assistants;
- no work contacts exported to marketing lists;
- no real deal/candidate/test facts, even if anonymised;
- no screenshots or visual imitation of employer materials;
- maintain dated authoring logs and source/provenance records;
- use neutral professional bio without employer logo or endorsement;
- document recusals if any customer/partner creates a conflict;
- pause the business if employment role changes and re-review obligations.

## Swiss structure, tax and social security

Swiss SME guidance describes self-employment by independence, working in one’s own name, own infrastructure and financial risk, and recommends recognition by the compensation office early in the activity. EU/EFTA nationals can generally start self-employment in Switzerland, subject to status/residence details.

A sole proprietorship is a reasonable validation-stage assumption because it has low setup cost and no minimum capital. Confirm trade-register naming/registration requirements, canton/permit implications and personal liability. Keep business accounts/expenses separate even though sole-proprietorship profit/wealth is taxed to the individual.

Swiss official guidance currently states:

- ordinary businesses generally become VAT-liable at CHF 100,000 worldwide taxable turnover;
- summary accounts can generally be used below CHF 500,000 turnover and business records should be retained for at least 10 years;
- self-employed AHV/IV/EO contributions and status are handled through the compensation office; rates depend on income and can change.

Confirm deductions, provisional payments, AHV status and cross-border sales with a Swiss fiduciary before launch.

## EU/UK/international digital VAT

Digital cases, video access and an add-in sold to consumers can be electronically supplied services. EU rules generally tax B2C electronic services where the consumer resides, and the non-Union OSS enables a non-EU business to register in one EU state for covered services. Record/evidence requirements can extend for years. The EU’s EUR 10,000 simplification is not a general safe harbour for a Swiss-only seller.

Use a reputable merchant of record during validation/early MVP. Contractually verify that it is the seller/MoR for the exact products, collects destination VAT/sales tax, handles invoices/refunds and supplies compliant reports. “Payment processor” and “merchant of record” are not synonyms.

UK and other country obligations require separate confirmation. A MoR reduces indirect-tax administration but does not remove the founder’s income, AHV, privacy, IP, advertising or employment duties.

## Consumer protection and refunds

Before checkout disclose:

- seller/MoR identity and contact details;
- exact product, file/view restrictions, compatibility and access duration;
- total price/currency/taxes;
- delivery timing, preorder status and technical requirements;
- refund/withdrawal policy;
- licence restrictions and watermarks;
- no guarantee of interview/job outcome;
- complaint process.

EU consumers generally have online withdrawal rights; digital-content exceptions require specific conditions such as express consent to immediate supply and acknowledgement of the effect on withdrawal rights. Do not improvise a “no refunds after download” sentence. Have counsel/MoR configure checkout wording. Independently of minimum law, offer a fair defect-resolution/refund process to protect trust.

## Privacy and marketing

The Swiss FADP requires transparent information when personal data is collected. GDPR can apply to a Swiss company offering goods/services to EU residents or monitoring their behaviour. Build a data map covering:

- account/email and identity;
- orders, refunds, tax and invoicing;
- case progress, notes and downloads;
- watermarks and licence devices;
- support messages;
- partner attribution;
- analytics/cookies;
- vendors, processing locations, retention and deletion.

Required product work:

- accurate privacy notice with controller/contact, purposes, recipients, countries, retention and rights;
- data-processing agreements with vendors;
- deletion/export/account closure flow;
- breach process and processor register;
- separate optional marketing consent from transactional email;
- unsubscribe in every marketing email and suppression list;
- consent record with timestamp/text/version/source;
- cookie preferences that actually block optional analytics until consent and permit withdrawal.

EU guidance says analytics/market-research cookies generally require consent before they are set. Swiss cookie guidance emphasises transparent information and objection and can require prior consent for unexpected/high-risk processing. The current “OK” dismissal banner does not satisfy the product standard and should not be reused.

Avoid session replay in the authenticated case product. It adds privacy and trust cost while funnel events are enough.

## Intellectual property

### Creation policy

Every commercial case must be fictional and independently created. Public information can inform ranges/market realism only when its licence and use permit it. Do not copy:

- real interview tests or candidate submissions;
- employer or prior-employer models, decks, cases, processes or wording;
- competitor course/model files;
- real dataroom/CIM layouts or screenshots;
- third-party research charts without permission;
- company/university/fund logos or testimonials implying affiliation.

### Provenance register fields

- asset/content ID and version;
- author/reviewer/date/equipment/account;
- source URL/publication/date/licence;
- excerpted fact or range and transformation;
- fictionalisation method;
- AI assistance and human verification;
- similarity/plagiarism check;
- approval status and conflicts check.

### Protection

Copyright protects original expression in text, slides, models and code, but generic formulas, finance concepts and formatting conventions may receive limited protection. Use contractual licence terms, private source storage and release watermarks in addition to copyright notices. Registering a trademark for Sources & Uses may be possible, but it is a descriptive/common finance phrase and clearance/protectability must be assessed before investment.

The current repository is public and uses an inherited MIT licence. Do not place commercial content or add-in code there. A new private repository should have an explicit proprietary content/code policy and retain third-party notices for open-source dependencies.

Fictional company names/logos should be searched for conflicts and should not imitate real companies. Avoid “official,” “firm-approved,” “headhunter-approved” or logos of PE firms/schools without documented permission.

## Piracy and account sharing

### Risk by asset

| Asset             | Leakage likelihood | Commercial effect              | Proportionate control                                      |
| ----------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Entry drills/PDFs | Very high          | Severe because value is static | Low price, watermark, accept leakage                       |
| CIM slides        | High               | Medium                         | Auth viewer, page watermark, no source PDF                 |
| Blank/input Excel | High               | Medium                         | User watermark/metadata where safe, version IDs            |
| Completed model   | High               | High static value              | Release after completion, visible sheet watermark, updates |
| Videos            | Medium–high        | Medium                         | Auth streaming, account limits, no invasive DRM            |
| Rubric/debrief    | High               | Medium                         | Browser access, version updates                            |
| Add-in            | Medium             | Medium                         | Account/device validation; code remains inspectable        |

### Account policy

- one named user;
- normal simultaneous sessions allowed across browser/phone but anomaly monitored;
- add-in: maximum two active devices and self-service deactivation;
- progressive response to sharing: warning, reauthentication, temporary suspension, human review;
- no biometric or invasive fingerprinting;
- clear household/team restrictions;
- university seats/codes so legitimate groups do not share one account.

### Value that survives copying

- corrected/current case versions;
- timed progress and notes;
- cohort benchmark/calibration, with consent;
- quality assurance and errata support;
- trusted partner distribution;
- future cases/extensions;
- licence-backed add-in updates;
- buyer confidence that the solution is internally audited.

Static files will leak if the product succeeds. The business must remain worthwhile under that assumption.

## Risk classification

### Fatal until resolved

- employment/IP approval or boundary is inadequate;
- source provenance cannot demonstrate original, non-confidential creation;
- consumer checkout/tax seller-of-record arrangement is unclear;
- privacy tracking launches without valid control.

### Serious but manageable

- descriptive brand/trademark weakness;
- cross-border VAT and refund complexity;
- add-in workbook permissions/security/support;
- user-generated notes/progress retention;
- university/recruiter affiliation language;
- copied solution files/account sharing.

### Minor

- routine copyright notices;
- basic watermark customisation;
- record-retention automation at very low volume, provided a manual compliant process exists.

## Professional-confirmation checklist

1. Swiss employment/IP lawyer: contract, approval, duty/conflict, bio/marketing and content method.
2. Swiss fiduciary: sole proprietorship, AHV, deductions, canton tax, VAT threshold and records.
3. MoR/tax specialist if needed: exact product classification, EU/UK/other consumer VAT, invoice/refund roles.
4. Privacy counsel/template review: FADP/GDPR scope, cookies, transfers, retention and rights.
5. Trademark/IP counsel: brand clearance, terms, licence and source/public-content policy.
