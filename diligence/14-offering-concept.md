# Offering concept — Sources & Uses IC Simulation Series

Status: detailed product concept for validation. This is not an implementation specification or authorisation to build the platform.

## 1. The offering in one sentence

> A timed private-equity case simulation in which the candidate turns an imperfect European mid-market company pack into an LBO, an investment recommendation and an IC presentation, then compares the work against an audited solution and explicit decision-quality standards.

The product is not primarily a course. It is a realistic rehearsal environment with a structured debrief.

Working product name: **Sources & Uses — IC Simulation 01**. The name and all fictional company/fund names require clearance before commercial use.

## 2. The customer promise

The customer should finish with four concrete outcomes:

1. **A completed rehearsal:** they have performed the whole case within a realistic time limit rather than watched someone else do it.
2. **A diagnosis:** they know whether the principal weakness was modeling, commercial judgment, transaction reasoning, prioritisation or communication.
3. **A repeatable method:** they leave with a case-planning sheet, model checks and an IC communication structure reusable in a real interview.
4. **A realistic confidence level:** they know which parts are interview-ready and which require more practice.

The product must not promise an offer, placement, certification value, recruiter recognition or personalised professional advice.

## 3. Who it is for

### Primary customer

An analyst, consultant, transaction-services professional or finance student who:

- has a European PE interview or case round within approximately 2–12 weeks;
- already understands basic accounting and LBO concepts;
- wants practice integrating financial and commercial analysis;
- expects to submit a model, recommendation or presentation;
- can use desktop Excel independently.

### Not for

- someone learning accounting or Excel for the first time;
- someone looking for a complete PE recruiting curriculum;
- someone expecting firm-specific leaked questions or real interview tests;
- someone who needs live coaching or personal model review;
- an experienced investor seeking live-deal advice;
- anyone expecting a certificate to carry recruiting weight.

The landing page should make these exclusions visible. Turning away the wrong customer will reduce refunds and strengthen trust.

## 4. Product system

The offering begins as two connected products, not a library.

| Product                      | Purpose                                                  |            User commitment |      Price during validation |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------: | ---------------------------: |
| **Free Decision Diagnostic** | Demonstrate the judgment-first method and qualify demand |                 15 minutes |                         Free |
| **IC Simulation 01**         | Full model-to-recommendation rehearsal and debrief       | 3–4 hours including review | CHF 59 beta; CHF 79 standard |

The free diagnostic should be genuinely useful but should not attempt to teach the full case. The paid simulation should feel complete on its own; it must not depend on buying a subsequent module.

## 5. Flagship case concept

### Provisional fictional company

**Aurelis Compliance Services** is a placeholder name for an independently created, fictional German-headquartered provider of recurring inspection, maintenance and compliance services for distributed commercial assets across DACH and Benelux.

Illustrative company profile:

- approximately €35m–€45m revenue;
- mid-to-high teens adjusted EBITDA margin;
- mix of recurring contracted inspections, reactive services and small software/data fees;
- decentralised technician workforce operating through regional branches;
- meaningful customer concentration below an immediately disqualifying level;
- recent bolt-on acquisition that complicates like-for-like growth and EBITDA quality;
- management plan built on price increases, technician productivity and cross-selling;
- credible opportunity for buy-and-build, with execution and integration risk;
- potential sale by a founder/family shareholder to a mid-market sponsor.

Why this archetype works:

- it is more distinctive than another pure SaaS case;
- it supports recurring-revenue analysis without pretending all revenue is equivalent;
- it creates tension between labour intensity, pricing power and attractive cash conversion;
- it supports customer cohort, utilisation and regional analysis;
- it allows realistic debt capacity, capex, working-capital and bolt-on questions;
- it creates several defensible investment conclusions.

The commercial version must not copy a real company, transaction, lender package, model or interview test. Sector facts should be constructed from licensed public sources, transformed and recorded in a provenance ledger. The fictional company name and logo require trademark and similarity checks.

### Candidate role

The candidate is told:

> You are an investment professional at a fictional European mid-market fund. Your team must decide whether to submit a non-binding offer for Aurelis. You have two hours to review the materials, complete the required model outputs and prepare a preliminary recommendation for the investment committee.

No fictional fund brand is necessary. Avoid mimicking a known firm's language, templates or visual identity.

### Core decision tension

The case should not reduce to “calculate the IRR.” The central tension is:

> Does attractive headline growth and recurring compliance demand justify the entry valuation and leverage when organic growth quality, technician capacity, customer concentration and bolt-on integration remain uncertain?

The materials should support at least three defensible positions:

1. proceed at the indicated valuation, subject to targeted diligence;
2. proceed only at a lower price or with different financing assumptions;
3. do not proceed because the underwriting depends on insufficiently evidenced operational improvement.

The scoring system rewards evidence and internal consistency, not selection of a predetermined answer.

## 6. The candidate package

### A. Candidate instructions — two pages

The instructions state:

- the candidate's role and decision date;
- exact time allowance and suggested allocation;
- permitted tools and assumptions;
- required deliverables;
- definitions of adjusted EBITDA, cash, debt and transaction perimeter;
- whether external research is allowed;
- how to handle missing information;
- what will and will not be scored;
- submission-readiness checklist.

The case should never rely on hidden instructions. Difficulty comes from prioritisation and judgment.

### B. Browser-viewed case pack — approximately 24–30 pages

Proposed sections:

1. transaction overview and process context;
2. company history, footprint and ownership;
3. service lines and revenue model;
4. customer segments and top-customer development;
5. retention, contract structure and revenue recurrence;
6. market drivers and competitive landscape;
7. branch and technician operating KPIs;
8. historical income statement and cash-flow summary;
9. quality-of-earnings adjustments;
10. management plan and operational initiatives;
11. transaction, financing and exit assumptions;
12. selected risks and incomplete diligence findings.

Information should be imperfect but coherent. Examples of legitimate ambiguity include different management and adviser definitions of recurring revenue, an EBITDA adjustment with debatable persistence, or a management plan that exceeds historic hiring capacity. Accidental contradictions are defects, not realism.

### C. Downloadable inputs workbook

An unlocked workbook contains only information candidates would reasonably receive in machine-readable form:

- three historical years by service line and geography;
- customer/revenue concentration data;
- branch and technician KPIs;
- working-capital, capex and cash-flow history;
- management projections;
- transaction and debt assumptions.

It must contain no hidden solution formulas, comments, author metadata or solution sheets.

### D. Blank model workbook

The workbook provides structure without solving the case:

- cover/instructions;
- assumptions;
- historicals and operating case;
- transaction sources and uses;
- debt schedule;
- returns summary;
- sensitivities;
- checks.

The candidate should still construct the important formulas. The workbook may provide row labels and dates, but not opaque LBO logic. An advanced future case can provide less scaffolding.

### E. Recommendation template

One page with the following headings:

- recommendation and proposed conditions;
- investment thesis;
- principal risks/disconfirming evidence;
- value-creation plan;
- returns and downside;
- next diligence priorities.

The template should constrain length. It must not become a fill-in-the-blanks answer key.

### F. Five-slide IC template

1. recommendation and key terms;
2. business quality and market position;
3. underwriting and value creation;
4. returns, leverage and downside;
5. risks, open questions and next steps.

Candidates can change the structure if their communication is clearer. The debrief should explain why description without a decision is weak.

## 7. The simulation experience

### Suggested time allocation

| Stage                     |  Suggested time | Candidate action                                                   |
| ------------------------- | --------------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Frame the decision        |      10 minutes | Read instructions, identify outputs and write initial hypotheses   |
| Review the case pack      |      25 minutes | Extract commercial drivers, risks and missing information          |
| Build and check the model |      55 minutes | Complete transaction, debt, operating and returns outputs          |
| Decide                    |      10 minutes | Select recommendation and conditions; reconcile narrative to model |
| Communicate               |      20 minutes | Complete one-page recommendation and five-slide IC pack            |
| Final review              |      10 minutes | Run checks, remove inconsistencies and prepare files               |
| **Total**                 | **120 minutes** |                                                                    |

The timer is a planning tool, not proctoring. Provide one pause for accessibility or unexpected interruption and a clearly labelled untimed practice mode. Only a completed timed attempt should be compared with a timed cohort.

### Experience flow

```text
Product preview
    -> readiness check
    -> purchase / access
    -> case briefing and mode selection
    -> two-hour case room
    -> decision snapshot and self-attestation
    -> solution unlock
    -> model and IC debrief
    -> self-score and benchmark
    -> extension question / next practice recommendation
```

### Case-room screen

```text
+---------------------------------------------------------------+
| Aurelis Compliance Services | 01:34 remaining | Notes | Help   |
+------------------+--------------------------------------------+
| Page thumbnails  |                                            |
| 01 Transaction   |              Case page                     |
| 02 Company       |                                            |
| 03 Customers     |         zoom / fit / page controls         |
| ...              |                                            |
+------------------+--------------------------------------------+
| Downloads: Inputs.xlsx | Blank Model.xlsx | IC Template.pptx  |
+---------------------------------------------------------------+
```

The browser does not need to reproduce a complex virtual dataroom. It needs fast navigation, legible pages, a timer, private notes and reliable downloads. Artificial friction would damage the simulation.

### Decision snapshot

Before unlocking the answer, the candidate records a short structured snapshot:

- proceed / conditional proceed / decline;
- maximum entry valuation or price condition;
- base-case MOIC/IRR range;
- opening and exit leverage;
- top three thesis points;
- top three risks;
- most important unanswered diligence question.

No model or memo upload is required initially. This reduces privacy and storage risk. The structured snapshot supports product analytics and future cohort comparisons without claiming to score the workbook automatically.

## 8. The debrief product

The debrief is the principal paid value. It should require as much editorial care as the case itself.

### A. Seven-minute decision replay

The user first sees the minimum decision path:

1. what was known;
2. what mattered most;
3. what did not deserve much time;
4. which assumptions drove the recommendation;
5. which evidence should have reduced confidence.

This prevents the debrief from becoming a formula walkthrough disconnected from judgment.

### B. Audited solution model

Provide a downloadable model with:

- clean formulas and professional formatting;
- visible checks;
- base and downside cases;
- debt repayment and cash-flow logic;
- sensitivity tables;
- comments only where they teach a decision or modeling principle;
- a model-change log matching the released case version.

The solution represents one coherent approach, not the only acceptable model structure.

### C. Model comparison guide

Instead of automated workbook auditing, give the candidate a deterministic comparison checklist:

- sources and uses reconciliation;
- EBITDA and adjustment treatment;
- cash-flow conversion;
- minimum cash and debt repayment;
- interest convention;
- entry/exit enterprise-to-equity bridge;
- MOIC and IRR;
- sensitivity direction;
- consistency between model and recommendation.

### D. Strong and weak decision packages

Show two fully worked examples:

- **strong answer:** concise, evidence-linked, explicit about conditions and uncertainty;
- **plausible but weak answer:** numerically reasonable yet descriptive, overconfident or inconsistent with the downside.

Annotate why each passage or slide helps or hurts an IC decision. Do not create a caricatured “bad answer.” The weak answer should resemble errors competent candidates actually make.

### E. Alternative valid answer

Include at least one credible answer reaching a different recommendation from the primary solution. Explain which assumptions and risk tolerance make it defensible. This is crucial to proving that the product tests judgment rather than answer matching.

### F. Common mistakes

Examples:

- treating all contracted revenue as equally recurring;
- accepting every EBITDA adjustment;
- using exit multiple expansion without justification;
- confusing revenue growth with technician capacity;
- showing a high base-case IRR without a liquidity/downside view;
- listing risks without explaining decision impact;
- presenting five descriptive slides and no explicit recommendation;
- building an elaborate model while leaving required outputs incomplete;
- formatting cleanly but failing fundamental checks.

## 9. Scoring system

The initial score is a structured self-assessment. It is not AI-generated and must not be marketed as an objective hiring prediction.

| Dimension                              |  Weight | What strong performance demonstrates                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------- | ------: | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Model integrity and prioritisation     |      25 | Correct core mechanics, visible checks, appropriate scope and consistent outputs                            |
| Commercial analysis                    |      20 | Identifies true growth/quality drivers, challenges evidence and connects operations to forecasts            |
| Transaction, debt and returns judgment |      20 | Understands price, leverage, cash generation, downside and sensitivity rather than quoting IRR alone        |
| Investment decision quality            |      20 | Makes an explicit, conditional recommendation with thesis, risks, disconfirming evidence and next diligence |
| IC communication                       |      15 | Concise, decision-led, internally consistent and professionally presented                                   |
| **Total**                              | **100** |                                                                                                             |

Each dimension should contain 4–6 observable criteria scored on a four-level scale:

- **0 — missing or materially wrong**;
- **1 — partially complete but decision-weak**;
- **2 — competent interview standard**;
- **3 — strong and decision-useful**.

The product can translate the weighted result into developmental bands:

- **85–100:** strong integrated performance;
- **70–84:** credible, with identifiable improvement areas;
- **55–69:** incomplete integration despite some correct components;
- **below 55:** repeat core workflow before attempting a harder case.

These bands are editorial standards at launch, not empirical percentiles. Cohort percentiles should appear only after at least 30 comparable, consenting completions on the same case version. Always display cohort definition, sample size and version; never imply recruiter validation.

### Example rubric anchor

**Investment recommendation — 6 weighted points**

- 0: no recommendation or recommendation contradicts the submitted analysis;
- 2: recommendation is stated but unconditional or weakly supported;
- 4: recommendation is supported by key commercial and returns evidence with major conditions;
- 6: recommendation is decision-ready, distinguishes facts from assumptions, identifies disconfirming evidence and states what would change the decision.

## 10. Free Decision Diagnostic

The free product uses a small, independently understandable excerpt inspired by the same reasoning style, not pages cut from the middle of the paid case.

### Format

- two-page fictional company snapshot;
- six questions;
- one small debt/returns calculation;
- one forced recommendation;
- 15-minute timer;
- immediate two-page debrief;
- optional email delivery, with marketing consent separated.

### Questions

1. Which revenue stream appears most defensible, and why?
2. Which management claim needs the strongest challenge?
3. What single KPI would you request before underwriting growth?
4. Calculate indicative debt paydown from the supplied figures.
5. Identify the most important downside interaction.
6. Proceed, proceed conditionally or decline—and give three reasons.

The diagnostic ends with a result such as “strongest in mechanics; less evidence of commercial challenge,” followed by a preview of the full case. It should not manufacture a psychologically manipulative personality type.

## 11. Product page concept

### Hero

**Headline:** Rehearse the full investment decision—not just the LBO.

**Subhead:** A two-hour European mid-market PE simulation combining commercial analysis, modeling, downside judgment and IC communication, followed by an audited solution and explicit scoring rubric.

**Primary action:** Try the free 15-minute diagnostic.

**Secondary action:** Preview the full simulation.

### Proof immediately below the hero

- exact screenshot of the fictional case room;
- one redacted/partial model screenshot;
- excerpt of the rubric;
- example of a strong versus weak recommendation paragraph;
- “original fictional company and independently audited model” statement;
- precise time, prerequisites, files and price.

Avoid fabricated fund logos, placement statistics, artificial countdown timers, vague “industry secrets” or claims that the product is used by firms unless documented.

### Product-page sequence

1. the situation: “your case is in two weeks”;
2. what the candidate will actually do;
3. interactive preview/screenshots;
4. exact files and debrief components;
5. what is scored;
6. who it is and is not for;
7. comparison with a modeling course, free AI practice and live coaching;
8. originality and quality process;
9. access, refund and technical requirements;
10. price and start action.

## 13. Access and content rules

### Browser-only during the attempt

- CIM/case pages;
- private notes;
- timer and progress;
- pre-completion rubric headings;
- solution/debrief pages until unlock.

### Downloadable at the start

- candidate instructions/printable checklist;
- structured input workbook;
- blank model;
- recommendation and presentation templates.

### Downloadable after completion

- solution model;
- model comparison checklist;
- concise case summary for later revision.

### Browser-first after completion

- annotated strong/weak answers;
- video/written debrief;
- detailed scoring anchors;
- alternative valid recommendation;
- cohort benchmark when eligible.

View-only restrictions should protect experience sequencing, not hold essential learning artifacts hostage. A visible, partially masked email/order watermark is proportionate. Screenshot prevention and invasive DRM are not.

## 14. Brand and visual character

The presentation should feel like an independent professional assessment product, not a copied investment-firm template.

- restrained typography and neutral colour palette;
- dense enough for professional information, but highly legible;
- consistent chart grammar and source/assumption labels;
- no marble, skyscraper or “Wall Street lifestyle” imagery;
- no imitation firm logos, tombstones or university badges;
- clear labels for facts, management claims, founder assumptions and candidate assumptions;
- plain English, with European transaction terminology explained where necessary.

The tone is calm, exacting and non-performative: “Here is the evidence; make the decision.”

## 15. Support model

The product must remain operationally light:

- self-service access and password reset after the validation phase;
- in-product issue report carrying case version/page reference;
- concise technical guide for supported Excel versions;
- knowledge-base answers for download, timer, unlock and workbook issues;
- response target of two business days, not real-time chat;
- no case hints during a timed attempt;
- published errata and version notes;
- no free personal review by the founder.

Track support minutes per buyer. If recurring support exceeds approximately 15 minutes per order after the beta, simplify the product before expanding it.

## 16. Quality and trust signals

Trust must come from verifiable process rather than employer-derived authority.

Display:

- “100% fictional company and transaction”;
- case version and last audit date;
- concise description of the independent review process;
- numerical checks performed;
- transparent errata history;
- reviewer biography limited to public, permissible experience;
- clear non-affiliation statement;
- sample provenance explanation without exposing authoring internals unnecessarily.

Do not display unverifiable “real PE test” language. A more credible phrase is “designed to reproduce the decisions and time constraints common to European mid-market case exercises.”

## 17. Why this is harder to copy than a case PDF

No individual feature is a strong moat. The edge comes from the integrated system:

```text
Original coherent case
    + independently audited model
    + multiple valid decision paths
    + observable scoring anchors
    + strong/weak submission comparisons
    + timed completion data
    + version-specific cohort benchmarks
    + trusted distribution partners
```

A competitor or AI can generate a fictional CIM. It is harder to produce and maintain a coherent cross-file assessment, demonstrate that candidates complete it in the intended time, and build trusted version-specific comparison data.

## 18. First-case success criteria

The offering concept is supported only if the paid test demonstrates:

- at least 8 paid orders from 150 qualified visitors or 15 orders overall without paid acquisition;
- at least 10 buyers start the case;
- at least 40% of starters complete and unlock the debrief;
- median realism and usefulness of at least 8/10;
- at least 60% of completers can name a distinctive paid outcome unaided;
- no unresolved critical numerical defect;
- refund or serious-defect requests no higher than 8%;
- two partners send real, tracked qualified traffic;
- production and independent audit fit inside the 120-hour validation cap or show a credible path below it.

Failure means one focused revision at most. It does not authorise a larger library, coaching offer, add-in or broad finance course.

## 19. What the prototype must show

The validation prototype needs only enough fidelity to make the purchase decision real:

1. landing-page hero and product description;
2. complete free diagnostic;
3. 8–12 finished pages from the fictional case pack;
4. first page of each downloadable template;
5. case-room clickable prototype with timer and navigation;
6. one complete rubric dimension;
7. one strong/weak recommendation comparison;
8. 90-second narrated walkthrough;
9. actual CHF 59/79 checkout or refundable preorder;
10. optional five-command Excel add-in video shown as a future concept, clearly not included.

Do not build accounts, file uploads, automated scoring, device licensing or the production viewer to run this test.

## 20. Final product judgment

The strongest version of Sources & Uses is not “the European Peak Frameworks.” It is a narrower product category:

> **A self-service IC simulation and performance diagnostic for candidates who already know the theory.**

That focus sacrifices the beginner market and content-volume comparisons. In return, it gives the product a plausible reason to exist alongside courses, coaches, YouTube and AI.

If customers treat IC Simulation 01 as merely another downloadable case, the concept has failed. If they describe it as the closest rehearsal they found for turning imperfect information into a defensible investment decision, it has earned the right to become a series.
