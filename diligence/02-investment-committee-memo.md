# Deliverable 2 — Investment-committee memo

## Executive summary

Sources & Uses should not be funded as a platform today. It should receive a small option-value budget to validate one high-quality product: a timed, original European mid-market investment case that combines modeling, commercial judgment and IC communication.

The opportunity is commercially plausible as a side business, not yet as a standalone company. Established competitors prove willingness to pay but also set a high quality and distribution bar. Peak Frameworks has deep content, recruiter distribution, creator authority and a $497 product. Wall Street Oasis has a community/SEO moat and a $297 PE course. BIWS reports 56,763 paid accounts and 145+ institutional clients. Multiple Expansion gives away sophisticated models. Break Into PE already occupies the Europe-specific recruiting position and sells coaching-led outcomes. Low-cost Excel add-ins undercut a standalone productivity-tool thesis.

The project’s defensible edge can only come from product quality and an integrated assessment experience: audited fictional data, realistic ambiguity, calibrated scoring and professional artifacts. Those are laborious to produce. The founder’s fit is strong, but that strength becomes a liability if employment separation and content provenance are not formalised.

**IC decision: validate narrowly, then return to committee.** Cash cap CHF 3,000; founder-time cap 120 hours; six-to-eight weeks; one paid case; no production add-in; no broad platform.

## Market and customer

The customer is not “everyone interested in PE.” The initial customer is an English-speaking analyst, consultant, finance master’s student or adjacent transaction professional who has an actual European PE process in the next 2–12 weeks and expects a timed case/model/memo exercise.

Their problem is not lack of definitions. It is the inability to rehearse a coherent, realistic process and know whether their output is decision-useful. Urgency rises sharply only after an interview invitation. This makes the business seasonal and event-driven, but also supports willingness to pay.

**Estimate:** 6,000–15,000 people annually are plausibly in the initial English-language European serviceable segment. This is a constructed range, not a measured census. It is grounded in the concentration of European PE recruitment, the number of relevant finance programs and transition roles, and the fact that one London recruiter report profiles about 1,500 analysts/associates across finance and professional services. The broad European PE ecosystem is large—Invest Europe counted 29,338 active PE-backed portfolio companies in 2024—but that does not translate directly to interview candidates.

At CHF 100–150 annual spend, the initial serviceable revenue pool is roughly CHF 0.6m–2.25m. That can support a useful niche business but does not make venture-style platform investment rational. A three-year obtainable outcome of 300–1,000 annual buyers implies roughly CHF 40k–180k annual gross revenue depending on mix and price.

## Problem

Current alternatives solve pieces of the job:

- Peak/WSO/BIWS/WSP teach mechanics and provide many cases.
- Break Into PE provides Europe-specific guidance and human coaching.
- Multiple Expansion and YouTube provide excellent free mechanics.
- ChatGPT/Claude generate drills, explanations and mock memos instantly.
- friends, colleagues, alumni and coaches provide personalised feedback.

The under-served job is: **“Give me a credible rehearsal of the full case under realistic constraints, show me what good judgment looks like, and let me compare my output to explicit standards without booking a coach.”**

That job remains unproven. The validation must test it rather than assume it.

## Competition

Current product, pricing and market claims in this memo are linked and classified in the [evidence register](13-evidence-register.md). Provider performance claims are not treated as independently verified outcomes.

Peak is the modern benchmark but not the only serious threat. Its site currently advertises five three-statement LBO tests, seven paper LBOs, 10+ cases, 100+ videos, recruiter/resume distribution and a $497 limited price. Its media kit claims 10,000+ students and 40,000+ monthly visitors. WSO claims more than 5m monthly pageviews and sells a $297 PE course with nine tests and a large interview database. BIWS claims 144,189 YouTube subscribers, 56,763 paid users and 145+ institutional clients. Wall Street Prep says four of the top five global PE firms use its training. These are stronger moats than file count alone.

The most direct strategic warning is Break Into PE. It claims more than 100 European placements and an 83% 12-month placement rate, combines content with coaching, and explicitly says Europe is its differentiator. Those are self-reported claims, but the positioning alone invalidates “Europe” as an uncontested wedge.

## Product

### Recommended first product

One fictional European mid-market case in a defensible sector, with:

- a 15-minute free diagnostic;
- a 90–120 minute core simulation;
- browser-viewed CIM pages;
- downloadable raw historical/assumption inputs and blank workbook;
- required model outputs, one-page recommendation and five-slide IC pack;
- an audited solution model;
- an annotated strong answer and plausible-but-weak answer;
- scoring rubric with observable anchors;
- a 20–30 minute debrief;
- common mistakes and downside extensions.

Beta at CHF 59 to test conversion; standard at CHF 79 after quality proof. Do not call it a library. Do not create entry/core/premium tiers until the first case sells and is completed.

### Differentiation

The deeper wedge is not European logos, currency symbols or debt terminology. It is **calibrated realism**:

- incomplete data that still supports a decision;
- tension between model returns and commercial quality;
- explicit downside, information gaps and next diligence steps;
- professional model hygiene and concise IC communication;
- scoring based on evidence and decision quality, not matching one forecast;
- version-controlled, regression-tested case materials.

## Distribution

Distribution is the principal risk. The founder should not rely on a personal LinkedIn audience if that creates employment ambiguity. The first tests should be low-profile and attributable to the product brand:

1. 20–30 university finance/PE club approaches with a free diagnostic and unique link;
2. 10–15 small finance creators/newsletters with 20% tracked commission on first purchase;
3. 10 recruiters asked to critique/distribute the free diagnostic, not endorse the paid product;
4. four narrow search articles and two screen-recorded videos based on the case’s learning points;
5. direct interviews with 15–20 recent candidates.

University clubs can supply trust and clustered users but have annual committee turnover and high coordination cost. Recruiters may share a genuinely useful free diagnostic but are unlikely to procure material without a compliance/procurement case. YouTube has compounding search value but meaningful production burden. Paid acquisition should remain off until organic visitor-to-purchase conversion and refund rates are known.

## Business model and unit economics

Use a merchant of record during validation and early MVP. Lemon Squeezy currently advertises 5% + $0.50 per transaction and handles sales tax/VAT as merchant of record. That is materially more expensive than Swiss Stripe card processing but avoids disproportionate cross-border VAT operations.

Base steady-state model at CHF 95 blended AOV:

- payment/MoR and fixed fees: 6–7% at this ticket;
- refunds/chargebacks reserve: 4%;
- affiliate commissions: 20% of affiliate sale, assuming 25% of sales = 5% blended;
- direct variable delivery/email/storage: under 2%;
- contribution before founder time: approximately 82–85% of gross revenue.

At CHF 5,000 monthly gross revenue, approximately 53 orders/month are required. Under the base funnel (25% qualified visitor-to-lead and 4% lead-to-order), this requires roughly 5,300 qualified monthly visitors, not generic impressions. After variable fees and roughly CHF 500–1,000 of monthly operating costs, pre-tax founder income is around CHF 3,200–3,700 before valuing 8–15 support/content hours. See the economics appendix for scenarios.

Subscriptions are not justified at launch: interview need is episodic and the product lacks a recurring outcome. “Lifetime access” creates an indefinite maintenance obligation and weakens future monetisation. Prefer one-time access to the purchased case plus 12 months of material updates. Annual library updates can be tested only after three or more cases exist.

## Excel add-in

The add-in does not belong in the MVP. Office.js can support cross-platform task panes, ribbon commands, formatting and direct precedent/dependent APIs. Custom shortcuts exist on modern Excel across Windows, Mac and web, but require shared runtime support and can conflict with existing shortcuts. The application is a hosted web app, so authentication, availability and updates become ongoing services.

Standalone economics are weak. QuickCel sells formatting or tracing at $3/month and all features at $6/month. Arixcel sells Windows formula-review tooling at £2.75 per user-month. Macabacus is much deeper at $200/year basic. An interview-focused subset can create bundle appeal, but candidates may use it briefly, work devices may block add-ins, and some model tests explicitly ban them.

Use a prototype in the offer test. Build only after the paid case validates and the prototype changes observed premium selection—not merely survey enthusiasm.

## Online viewer

The viewer improves simulation and entitlement control but is not a moat. For Phase 2, render CIM PDF pages to images server-side, store them privately and serve authorised, short-lived page responses with a visible user watermark. Downloadable controls can be removed, but screenshots and browser extraction remain possible.

Keep candidate inputs and blank models downloadable. Keep CIM pages and pre-completion solutions browser-only. Unlock the solution after completion/self-attestation; make the completed model downloadable because working through formulas is part of the paid learning outcome. Do not force sequential case unlocks until user behaviour shows it helps.

## Legal and employment considerations

Before taking money, the founder should obtain professional confirmation on the employment contract, duty of loyalty, side-activity notice/approval, IP assignment and conflict boundaries. Operate only on personal time, equipment, accounts and source materials. Maintain a provenance register for every case datum, logo, image, template and explanation. Never reuse real tests or employer conventions that are not public/generic.

Swiss official guidance says self-employed status depends on independence and financial risk and recommends early recognition by the compensation office. Swiss VAT liability generally begins at CHF 100,000 of worldwide taxable turnover, but EU consumer digital-service VAT can arise separately; a merchant of record is the pragmatic early solution. The current site’s tracking consent is not adequate and should be disabled until rebuilt.

## Founder fit

Founder-market fit is strong on content quality and weak/unknown on distribution. The founder can credibly produce the integrated investment process that generic educators struggle to replicate. However, credibility may be difficult to market while staying separate from employment. Brand-led, evidence-led distribution can work, but anonymous positioning reduces trust. This tension is a serious validation question, not a copywriting issue.

## Final answer

**Would I invest my own time and money?** Yes—up to CHF 3,000 and 120 hours to buy evidence. No—I would not yet commit the several hundred hours required for the platform or add-in. If the validation gates fail, I would stop and reuse only the case-quality process and selected public content, not pivot reflexively into a broader finance course.
